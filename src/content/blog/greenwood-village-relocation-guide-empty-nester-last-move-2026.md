<p>Most relocation searches optimize for the next five years. A last-move search optimizes for the next twenty-five. That single difference changes what matters: single-level living beats a grand staircase, an HOA that handles the roof beats a half-acre of turf, and proximity to a trail you will walk daily beats proximity to an office you visit twice a week.</p>

<p>Greenwood Village sits at the center of a lot of those searches. For buyers <a href="/relocation">relocating to the Denver Metro</a> with a buy-once mindset, <a href="/neighborhoods/greenwood-village">Greenwood Village</a> offers something unusual: estate privacy, Denver Tech Center access, the Highline Canal Trail out the door, and a small but real stock of lock-and-leave condos — all inside one Arapahoe County ZIP map.</p>

<p>This guide covers the 2026 price tiers, the low-maintenance inventory most portals bury, the market pace, and the remote-buying process. The numbers come from REcolorado MLS data pulled May 3, 2026, plus the DMAR April 2026 Market Trends Report for metro context.</p>

<h2 id="why-gv">Why Do Empty-Nesters Pick Greenwood Village, Colorado for a Last Move?</h2>

<p>The first reason is that the lifestyle scales down without leaving. A buyer can land in a 7,000-square-foot Preserve estate at 58, sell it at 70, and move into a Landmark tower condo without changing grocery stores. Few Denver suburbs let a household right-size twice inside the same square mile. The sellers making that exact second move are profiled in the <a href="/blog/greenwood-village-empty-nester-right-sizing-math-2026">Greenwood Village right-sizing math post</a> — this guide is the buy side of the same market.</p>

<p>The second reason is the amenity profile. The Highline Canal Trail runs through the village, flat and shaded — a daily-walk asset, not a weekend one. The Marjorie Perry Nature Preserve, Westlands Park, and the Curtis Center for the Arts sit inside village limits. Fiddler's Green Amphitheatre is a summer-concert walk or short drive for much of the village. Dining and medical access cluster along the Denver Tech Center corridor minutes away.</p>

<p>The third reason is financial. Buyers arriving from California, Texas, or the East Coast often arrive with substantial equity. Research from the <a href="https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Association of Realtors</a> consistently shows older repeat buyers carry the highest all-cash share of any buyer group, and cash buyers do well in a market that punishes mispricing — more on that below. There is also a tax clock worth knowing: Colorado's senior property-tax exemption generally requires ten consecutive years of owner-occupancy, per the <a href="https://cdola.colorado.gov" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Colorado Department of Local Affairs</a>. A last move made at 62 starts that clock now. Confirm current rules with the county assessor, since recent legislation has adjusted portability for some sellers.</p>

<p>Read the ladder bottom-up if you are planning a last move. The $3M+ tier is the Greenwood Village of magazine spreads — 7,400 median finished square feet on estate lots. It is also a tier that demands staff-level grounds care, which is exactly what many relocating empty-nesters are leaving behind. The $1M–$2M band is the village's true center: 23 of the 55 active listings, a $1,450,000 median list price, and homes that are large without being burdensome. The under-$1M tier is small, but it exists — and it is the tier nobody associates with Greenwood Village.</p>

<h2 id="low-maintenance">Where Does Greenwood Village Hide Its Low-Maintenance Inventory?</h2>

<p>The lock-and-leave question is the one Jacob Stark hears most from relocating empty-nesters, and the honest answer is: the inventory is real but thin. As of May 3, 2026, twelve active listings sat under $1 million. Most were condos along the South Yosemite Street corridor on the Denver Tech Center edge — units from $318,900 to $460,000, typically 900 to 1,300 finished square feet, with the HOA carrying the roof, exterior, and grounds.</p>

<p>The Landmark towers raise the same formula to the luxury tier. An active Landmark unit listed at $590,000 with 927 finished square feet in early May. At the other end, a 1,809-square-foot, two-bedroom Landmark condo closed at $1,500,000 in April 2026 — 18 days in MLS, 97 percent of list. That sale is the proof point for the last-move thesis: a buyer paid single-family money for a condo specifically to own Greenwood Village without owning a lawn.</p>

<p>Between the condo tier and the estates sits the scarcest product: patio homes and smaller single-level ranches in the $1M–$2M band. They appear in ones and twos, and they move. A 2,762-finished-square-foot ranch on South Florence Street closed at $1,065,000 in April at 100 percent of list with zero days of market exposure beyond its listing week. When this product type fits, the buyer who has financing and search criteria ready before the listing goes live is the buyer who gets it.</p>

<h2 id="market-pace">How Fast Does the Greenwood Village Market Move in 2026?</h2>

<p>April 2026 tells a two-sided story. The 16 closed sales carried a 16-day median time in MLS and a 96 percent median close-to-original-list ratio. Several closed at full ask. For well-priced homes, Greenwood Village is a fast market despite its price point — the metro slowdown stories you may have read do not describe this corner of Arapahoe County.</p>

<p>The other side: 7 Greenwood Village listings expired unsold in April after a 57-day median sit, and the active pool carried a 39-day median time in MLS against the closed pool's 16. The <a href="https://www.dmarealtors.com/market-trends" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DMAR April 2026 Market Trends Report</a> put the Denver Metro median at $605,000 — Greenwood Village trades at roughly two and a half times that, and at this tier the spread between priced-right and priced-wrong is wide. Buyers should read an aged listing as a negotiation opening, not a defect. One April sale closed at 82 percent of its original list price after a long sit.</p>

<p>For a relocating buyer, the practical takeaway is to move decisively on fresh, well-priced inventory and patiently on stale inventory. Jacob Stark builds that split into the search itself — flagging which Greenwood Village listings price against the comparable record and which are testing the market. Buyers weighing the lower-priced DTC-access alternative can compare the <a href="/blog/lone-tree-relocation-guide-dtc-tech-professionals-2026">Lone Tree relocation guide</a>; the value-versus-cost question is covered honestly in <a href="/blog/greenwood-village-worth-the-price-tag-relocation-2026">Is Greenwood Village Worth the Price Tag?</a></p>

<p><strong>Step 1 — Pick the tier before you tour.</strong> A Landmark condo and a Preserve estate are not the same search. Decide the maintenance load first; the tier, the budget, and the tour list follow from it.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What is the median home price in Greenwood Village, Colorado in 2026?</h3>
<p>The median close price in Greenwood Village was $1,541,750 across 16 closed residential transactions in April 2026, based on REcolorado MLS data pulled May 3, 2026. The median close-to-original-list ratio was 96 percent and the median days in MLS was 16. Active inventory carried a $1,600,000 median list price across 55 listings. For context, DMAR reported a $605,000 Denver Metro median for April 2026.</p>

<h3>Does Greenwood Village have low-maintenance homes for relocating empty-nesters?</h3>
<p>Yes, but the inventory is thin. As of May 3, 2026, Greenwood Village carried 12 active listings under $1 million, mostly condos along the South Yosemite Street corridor near the Denver Tech Center at a $417,500 median list price. The Landmark towers add luxury lock-and-leave units, including a 1,809-square-foot condo that closed at $1,500,000 in April 2026. Patio-home and ranch options inside the $1M to $2M band appear in small numbers and move quickly.</p>

<h3>Is Greenwood Village expensive compared to other Denver suburbs?</h3>
<p>Yes. Greenwood Village's April 2026 median close price of $1,541,750 ran roughly two and a half times the $605,000 Denver Metro median reported by DMAR.
