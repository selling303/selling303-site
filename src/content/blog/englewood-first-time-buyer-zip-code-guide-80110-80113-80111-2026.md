<h2 id="three-markets">Why Englewood is three markets, not one</h2>

<p>Englewood is a small, landlocked Arapahoe County city of roughly 33,000 residents, sitting directly south of Denver between Sheridan and Cherry Hills Village. The city's physical footprint is compact — about seven square miles — and the residential ZIP map cleaves it into three meaningfully different markets.</p>

<p>80110 covers the west and central core. The South Broadway corridor runs north-south through 80110. It carries the light-rail line, the historic downtown shopping district, and the densest concentration of mid-century bungalows, post-war ranches, and converted small-multifamily homes. The housing stock skews oldest in 80110. Most homes were built between 1945 and 1965, with pockets of 1920s-and-earlier bungalows near downtown. Lots are smaller and grid-pattern. The entry-tier price point is the lowest of the three ZIPs.</p>

<p>80113 covers the east and northeast residential core. The dominant pattern is mid-century ranch and split-level single-family on slightly larger lots. The housing stock concentrates in the 1955 to 1975 build era. The northeast corner of 80113 sits up against the Cherry Hills Village boundary. That adjacency lifts the upper end of the ZIP meaningfully. The mid-tier ranges sit above 80110 but well below 80111's single-family floor. Walkability is concentrated near Hampden and South University. The rest of the ZIP is car-oriented residential.</p>

<p>80111 covers the southeast band. It extends well beyond the Englewood city limits into Greenwood Village, Centennial, and unincorporated Arapahoe County. The housing stock inside the actual City of Englewood portion of 80111 is mostly 1965 to 1990 single-family. The non-Englewood-city portion of 80111 carries a mix of 1990-to-present townhomes and condos near the Denver Tech Center plus larger single-family on bigger lots. The Tech Center light-rail proximity and the Inverness corporate corridor pull the single-family floor up. The condo and townhome inventory creates a bimodal entry point. This is the ZIP that confuses first-time buyers the most.</p>

<h2 id="80111-caveat">The 80111 caveat — when Englewood is only the postal address</h2>

<p>The single most common Englewood first-time-buyer mistake Jacob sees in 2026 is treating an 80111 listing as an "Englewood" listing without verifying the actual city. The 80111 ZIP code boundary extends well past the City of Englewood limits. A substantial share of 80111 inventory is physically inside Greenwood Village or Centennial. A smaller share sits in unincorporated Arapahoe County.</p>

<p>The implications for a first-time buyer are real and material. Property-tax mill levies vary between Englewood, Greenwood Village, and unincorporated Arapahoe County. School-attendance boundaries don't follow the ZIP code. They follow the actual school district and attendance zone. Municipal services (trash, snow removal, code enforcement, building permits) come from the actual city. HOA structures and metropolitan-district fees can differ noticeably between a Greenwood Village address and an Englewood city address — even when both carry an 80111 postal designation.</p>

<p>The two-minute verification check before writing an offer on any 80111 listing is to pull the parcel data on the Arapahoe County assessor's site. The assessor record lists the "Property City" as the actual incorporated jurisdiction (Englewood, Greenwood Village, or Centennial), not the postal city. The listing agent or buyer agent can also confirm. They check the property's municipal services and tax authority on the title commitment. First-time buyers who skip this step can end up under contract on a Greenwood Village home expecting Englewood services and mill levies.</p>

<h2 id="selection-questions">Four questions that pick the ZIP for you</h2>

<p>Most first-time buyers want a single answer for which ZIP to focus on. The reality is that the right ZIP is decided by four priority questions that Jacob walks every Englewood first-time buyer through during the buyer-consultation conversation.</p>

<p><strong>Question 1: How will you commute?</strong> If transit is non-negotiable or the goal is to operate without a second car, 80110 is the clear answer. Both RTD Southwest light-rail stations inside Englewood are in 80110. The Broadway bus corridor runs through it. If the commute is to the Denver Tech Center by car, 80111 wins on proximity (under 10 minutes typical). If the commute is to downtown Denver, the I-25 access from all three ZIPs is similar. The answer becomes about lifestyle rather than commute.</p>

<p><strong>Question 2: How important is the lot and the single-family feel?</strong> Buyers who want a real yard, a garage, and a single-family detached home should prioritize 80110 or 80113. 80113 offers slightly larger lots and a more residential feel. 80110 offers smaller lots but more character in the older housing stock. Buyers willing to consider attached housing (condo or townhome) and prioritizing newer construction can find the lowest entry point in 80111.</p>

<p><strong>Question 3: How much condition risk are you willing to absorb?</strong> 80110 carries the oldest housing stock and therefore the highest deferred-maintenance and update risk. A 1958 ranch in 80110 may need a roof, a furnace, an electrical panel update, or sewer line work in the first five years of ownership. The list price discount sometimes reflects that risk. Not always. 80113's 1955-to-1975 stock typically carries a middle level of condition risk. 80111's newer stock carries the lowest condition risk on a per-property basis.</p>

<p><strong>Question 4: Are you committed to Englewood specifically, or neutral on the actual city?</strong> First-time buyers who specifically want Englewood city services, Englewood school attendance, and the Englewood community identity should focus on 80110 and 80113. The city alignment is consistent in those ZIPs. Buyers neutral on the actual city — open to Greenwood Village or Centennial as long as the home and the location work — can broaden the search into 80111. The jurisdiction risk isn't a deal-breaker for that buyer. The decision should be conscious, not accidental.</p>

<p>For first-time buyers planning to eventually upgrade from an Englewood starter, the move-up pattern is well-documented. See <a href="/blog/move-up-englewood-to-parker-checklist-2026">the move-up buyer's checklist from a starter in Englewood to more space in Parker</a> and <a href="/blog/englewood-to-centennial-move-up-trade-off-2026">the Englewood-to-Centennial move-up trade math</a>. The starting ZIP inside Englewood doesn't lock the buyer into anything. But it does shape the equity build trajectory.</p>

<h2 id="what-fto-should-know">What every first-time buyer should know about all three ZIPs</h2>

<p>Three points apply regardless of which Englewood ZIP a first-time buyer ends up in.</p>

<p><strong>Inventory is thin compared to larger suburbs.</strong> Englewood closed 12 residential transactions in April 2026 across all three ZIPs combined (REcolorado MLS). That is a fraction of the volume in Highlands Ranch (131 closed) or Littleton (292 closed) in the same period. The practical effect for first-time buyers is that the active list at any given moment is short. A buyer waiting for the "right" property in a specific ZIP may wait several weeks between qualified options. Setting saved-search alerts across all three ZIPs broadens the menu without diluting the priority.</p>

<p><strong>FHA financing works in all three ZIPs but condition matters more.</strong> Most of Englewood's housing stock is older than the rest of the South Denver metro. FHA appraisals scrutinize peeling exterior paint, exposed electrical, roof condition, and certain plumbing fixtures more heavily than conventional appraisals. First-time buyers using FHA financing in 80110 or 80113 should expect a meaningful share of properties to require seller-completed condition repairs before closing. Homes built before 1978 also trigger lead-paint disclosure obligations. The total Colorado closing-cost framework covered in <a href="/blog/closing-costs-colorado-buyers-2026">the 2026 closing costs guide for Colorado buyers</a> applies across all three Englewood ZIPs.</p>

<p><strong>Investor and flipper activity is present.</strong> Englewood is a frequent target for fix-and-flip investors. The cosmetic-renovation arbitrage on a 1950s ranch in 80110 or 80113 is well understood. First-time buyers looking at a recently-renovated Englewood property should ask three things. Was the renovation permitted? What was actually replaced versus cosmetically updated? Were the major systems (furnace, water heater, electrical panel, roof) touched, or only the visible finishes? A pre-purchase inspection by a qualified home inspector is non-negotiable in Englewood across all three ZIPs.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What ZIP codes are in Englewood, Colorado?</h3>

<p>Englewood is covered by three residential ZIPs — 80110, 80113, and 80111 — plus two PO-box-only ZIPs (80150 and 80155). 80110 covers west and central Englewood along Broadway and Santa Fe, including the Englewood and Oxford light-rail stations. 80113 covers east and northeast Englewood from Logan Street east to South University Boulevard. It sits up against Cherry Hills Village. 80111 covers southeast Englewood and a wider band that extends into Greenwood Village and parts of Centennial. Many homes with an Englewood 80111 postal address are not inside Englewood city limits. Source: USPS ZIP boundaries and Arapahoe County GIS, confirmed against REcolorado MLS listings, April 2026.</p>

<h3>Which Englewood ZIP code is most affordable for first-time buyers?</h3>

<p>80110 is the most affordable Englewood ZIP for first-time buyer single-family inventory. Entry-tier single-family in 80110 typically lists in the $425,000 to $575,000 range. Most homes were built between 1945 and 1965 with finished square footage between 900 and 1,500. The light-rail corridor adds walkability and transit value. 80113 sits one tier above for first-time buyers. Its starter inventory typically runs $550,000 to $750,000. 80111 is bimodal. Condos and townhomes start around $325,000 to $475,000. Single-family in 80111 typically starts at $650,000 and runs past $1 million. Source: REcolorado MLS, City is Englewood, April 2026.</p>

<h3>Is 80111 really part of Englewood, Colorado?</h3>

<p>Partly. The 80111 ZIP carries an "Englewood" postal city designation. The ZIP boundary extends well outside the City of Englewood city limits. Many homes with an Englewood 80111 mailing address are inside Greenwood Village, Centennial, or unincorporated Arapahoe County. Englewood city itself is primarily covered by 80110 and 80113. First-time buyers shopping by postal city alone can end up under contract on a home in Greenwood Village. That home will carry a different property-tax mill levy, a different school-attendance boundary, and different municipal services. Confirm the actual jurisdiction on the listing's parcel data before writing an offer. Source: USPS, Arapahoe County GIS, and City of Englewood boundary maps.</p>

<h3>What is the median home price in Englewood, Colorado in April 2026?</h3>

<p>The median sale price across all residential transactions in Englewood, Colorado in April 2026 was approximately $605,000. Active and pending inventory carried a median list price of $595,000 and a typical asking price-per-finished-square-foot near $413. The April 2026 dataset included 12 closed residential transactions, 18 active listings, 10 pending listings, 5 coming-soon listings, 5 withdrawn listings, and 1 expired listing inside Englewood city boundaries. The overall transaction volume is meaningfully lower than larger South Denver suburbs because Englewood is a smaller, more landlocked city. The median sits in a price range broadly accessible to first-time buyers in the South Denver metro. Source: REcolorado MLS, City is Englewood, April 1 to 30, 2026.</p>

<h3>Which Englewood ZIP code has the best walkability for first-time buyers?</h3>

<p>80110 has the strongest walkability profile of the three Englewood ZIPs. The South Broadway corridor between Hampden Avenue and Yale Avenue runs through 80110. It connects to two RTD Southwest light-rail stations (Englewood Station and Oxford Station), the historic downtown Englewood shopping district, Englewood Recreation Center, Cushing Park, and a growing concentration of restaurants and small businesses. 80113 is primarily residential and car-oriented. Its walkability is concentrated near Hampden and South University Boulevard. 80111 is car-oriented in nearly all of its residential pockets. Walkability there is isolated to small mixed-use clusters near the Tech Center light-rail stops. First-time buyers who plan to commute by transit, walk to restaurants, or live without a second car will find the most natural fit in 80110.</p>
