<h2 id="math">What does $1M–$1.5M actually buy in Parker vs. Castle Pines?</h2>

<p>The headline trade at the $1M–$1.5M band is counterintuitive. Castle Pines costs more total at the median but less per finished square foot. The Castle Pines median close in this band ran $1,249,000 on 5,258 finished square feet, which works out to $229 per finished square foot. The Parker median close ran $1,199,724 on 5,076 finished square feet, or $244 per finished square foot. The Castle Pines premium is roughly $49,000 in absolute dollars and a $15 per finished square foot discount on per-foot economics.</p>

<p>The composition of that band differs by city. Parker $1M–$1.5M is mostly resale custom homes built between 2002 and 2015 on quarter-acre to two-acre lots — Stonegate Reserve, Pradera, Pinery's premium subdivisions, and the larger Stroh Ranch lots. Castle Pines $1M–$1.5M is a mix of Castle Pines Village resale and Canyons new construction at the Toll Brothers and Shea Homes move-up product lines, per the <a href="/blog/castle-pines-new-construction-builder-community-deep-dive-2026">Castle Pines builder community deep dive</a>. The Castle Pines per-foot discount lands because the newer Canyons floor plans are more efficient and the lots are slightly larger and more uniform than the equivalent Parker custom inventory.</p>

<h2 id="why">Why are Parker move-up buyers picking Castle Pines new construction?</h2>

<p>Four reasons come up consistently across Parker households Jacob Stark has worked with this spring. They are not equally weighted, and the rank order differs by family, but they show up in nearly every conversation.</p>

<p><strong>New construction warranty package.</strong> A Parker resale custom built in 2008 is a known quantity, but the roof, HVAC, water heater, and major systems are entering their second life. A Canyons new build at Toll Brothers or Shea Homes carries a 10-year structural warranty, a 2-year mechanical warranty, and a 1-year fit-and-finish warranty on top. For a household entering a $1.2M purchase, the warranty package is roughly $30,000 to $60,000 of avoided near-term maintenance over the first decade. That's the cleanest dollar-translatable reason the per-foot discount stops being the headline.</p>

<p><strong>Master-planned amenities.</strong> The Canyons HOA includes the Canyon House amenity center (pool, fitness, event space), 13 miles of soft-surface trail, and a curated streetscape on hilltop lots that overlook the Front Range. Parker resale at this band is mostly older custom subdivisions where the HOA is functional but not aspirational. The amenity stack is the second-largest reason Parker households cross-shop Castle Pines.</p>

<p><strong>Douglas County School District continuity.</strong> Both Parker and Castle Pines fall inside Douglas County School District (DCSD). A Parker family moving to Castle Pines does not change districts, which removes the single biggest friction point in most move-up decisions. The specific catchment elementary and middle schools change by subdivision, and Jacob Stark runs the subdivision-by-subdivision boundary check as part of the offer-prep workflow. But the district is the same district.</p>

<p><strong>Thin Parker $1M–$1.5M new-construction supply.</strong> The active Parker new-construction inventory at this tier is light. New builders in Parker tend to concentrate at the $750K–$1M attainable luxury band (per the <a href="/blog/spec-home-vs-custom-build-parker-2026">Parker new construction spec-vs-custom analysis</a>) and at the $2M+ custom band. The middle $1M–$1.5M new-construction band is sparse in Parker and dense in Castle Pines through The Canyons release cadence. Households that want both move-up square footage and new construction find more inventory in Castle Pines than in Parker at this price.</p>

<h2 id="hoa">How does the HOA and metro district stack compare?</h2>

<p>This is where a careful look helps. Castle Pines new construction at The Canyons carries a higher HOA than a typical Parker resale at this band, plus metro district mill levies that finance the infrastructure buildout. A Canyons monthly HOA typically runs $230 to $290 a month depending on lot section. That fee covers the amenity center, trail network, and common-area maintenance. The metro district mill levy adds roughly 30 to 60 mills on top of the base Douglas County rate during the bond-amortization window. Jacob Stark calculates that as $4,000 to $7,000 a year on a $1.2M assessed value, depending on the specific filing.</p>

<p>Parker resale at the $1M–$1.5M band typically carries a thinner HOA. $40 to $90 a month is common in Pradera and the larger Pinery lots. Acreage lots outside HOA boundaries carry none at all. Metro districts exist in some Parker subdivisions but are mostly past peak amortization in the older custom communities at this band. The cash flow comparison favors Parker resale on monthly fixed costs by roughly $250 to $450 a month and $4,000 to $7,000 a year on the metro district line. The Canyons buyer is paying the difference into infrastructure and amenities they use. The math works for households that actually use the trail network and amenity center. For households that don't, the Parker resale carrying cost is the cleaner number.</p>

<p>The way Jacob Stark frames the conversation: pull the seller's property disclosure, the HOA budget, and the metro district service plan before the inspection clears. The numbers are not hidden; they are just buried in three different documents that most buyers don't read until after the deal closes. Yesterday's post on the <a href="/blog/2026-notice-of-valuation-protest-playbook-south-denver">South Denver NOV protest playbook</a> covers the related Douglas County assessor mechanics if the move triggers a 2027 protest cycle on the new property.</p>

<h2 id="schools">What about school district continuity and lot size?</h2>

<p>Both Parker and Castle Pines sit inside Douglas County School District (DCSD), which removes the district-change friction that gates most move-up considerations. The specific catchments shift by subdivision, and that subdivision-level boundary check is the part most buyers skip and later regret. Jacob Stark runs the catchment confirmation against the DCSD boundary tool before any offer goes in, because the line can run mid-block in The Canyons where new sections are still being absorbed into existing elementary feeder patterns.</p>

<p>Lot size at the $1M–$1.5M band runs slightly larger in Castle Pines new construction than Parker resale on average. Canyons lots in the $1M–$1.5M product band typically span 9,000 to 14,000 square feet with hilltop or ridge positioning that adds usable outdoor space the flat numbers don't capture. Parker resale at the same band runs from 9,000 square feet on smaller Stonegate Reserve lots up to two-acre lots in the larger Pradera and outer Pinery sections. The Parker lot-size distribution is wider than Castle Pines; the high end of Parker exceeds anything The Canyons offers, but the typical lot is comparable.</p>

<p>Commute geometry differs slightly. Parker centers on I-25 and E-470, putting Denver Tech Center inside a 20-minute drive most weekday windows and downtown Denver inside 30 minutes. Castle Pines centers on I-25 a bit farther south, adding roughly 7 to 12 minutes to the same destinations depending on time of day. For a household with a DTC commute that runs a Castle Pines-to-DTC route already, the change is small. For a household with a downtown Denver commute, the change is meaningful and worth a test drive before the offer goes in.</p>

<h2 id="equity">How does the equity math work for a Parker seller?</h2>

<p>Most Parker households moving up to Castle Pines new construction at $1M–$1.5M are selling a 2018–2022 Parker home that's appreciated into the $750K–$950K range. The equity math follows a predictable pattern.</p>

<p>Start with the Parker sell side. A typical mid-tier Parker close in April 2026 sat around $850,000. The $1M–$1.5M band is the destination, not the sell point. Selling costs (commission, title fees, transfer fees, prep, concessions) typically run 6% of sale price, or about $51,000. A Parker owner who bought between 2018 and 2022 carries a $400,000 to $550,000 mortgage balance after a few years of paydown. Net usable equity lands around $250,000 to $400,000.</p>

<p>Now the Castle Pines buy side. A $1,249,000 Castle Pines new build at 20% down is $249,800. Closing costs and prepaids on the buy side typically run 2.5%, or about $31,225. New construction also typically requires a builder deposit of $20,000 to $40,000 at contract signing, separate from the down payment. That deposit releases at close. Cash to close lands around $281,025 plus the builder deposit, for a total liquidity need around $310,000 to $325,000 across the closing windows.</p>

<p>For a Parker seller with $250,000 to $400,000 of usable equity, the math typically works with $50,000 to $100,000 of surplus once both transactions clear.
