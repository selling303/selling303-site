<p>Three things jump off that matrix. Centennial buys you noticeably more finished square footage at the same price. Lakewood moves homes the fastest and prints the highest close-to-list ratio. Englewood pays for walkability and the older urban-edge stock with the smallest house and the longest days in MLS. None of those is wrong for every buyer — each describes a real trade-off the city is asking you to make at $750K.</p>

<h2 id="lakewood-flavor">Lakewood at $750K — west-metro foothills access, tight band</h2>

<p>$750,000 sits 27 percent above the citywide April 2026 Lakewood median of $589,450. That places this band squarely in upper-mid Lakewood — homes in the foothills-adjacent ZIPs (80228, 80227, 80215) more than the eastern flat-and-near-Belmar ZIPs (80214, 80232). Buyers shopping this band tour Green Mountain Park views, Bear Creek Trail proximity, and the larger lot sizes that come with Lakewood's hillier western neighborhoods.</p>

<p>The market behavior is tight. Five-day median time in MLS, 100 percent close-to-original-list ratio. That ratio is the tell. Lakewood buyers at $750K were not negotiating — when a home priced correctly hit the market, multiple offers landed within the first weekend and the seller held firm at or near list. The 19 active listings in the band as of May 3, 2026 represent thin shopping; expect to write quickly on the right home or watch it disappear. The same dynamic shows up at lower price tiers — the recent <a href="/blog/lakewood-listings-sit-on-market-pricing-strategy-2026">Lakewood expired-listing analysis</a> walks through the citywide 12-day closed median against a 90-day expired median, which mirrors the same priced-right-versus-not pattern at the $750K tier.</p>

<p>The trade-off Lakewood asks you to accept at this price point is finished square footage. 2,760 median sqft means most homes in the band are 1970s and 1980s ranches that have been updated, or smaller two-story homes on the west side. Newer construction at this band is rare in Lakewood — the city's new-build inventory clusters above $900K. Buyers who want move-in-ready new construction at $750K shop Centennial or Highlands Ranch instead.</p>

<h2 id="englewood-flavor">Englewood at $750K — urban-edge walkability, thin top tier</h2>

<p>$750,000 sits 25 percent above the citywide April 2026 Englewood median of $602,500. That places this band in top-of-market Englewood — the eastern 80113 mid-residential ZIP, the southeastern 80111 Tech Center border, and the occasional renovated home in the 80110 transit corridor. Englewood is the smallest of the three cities by total inventory volume, so this top tier turns over slowly. Only 5 homes closed in the $700K to $800K band in April 2026. The 21 active listings in the band tell a different story — the segment exists, buyers shop it, but each home tends to take longer to find its buyer.</p>

<p>The 25-day median days in MLS reflects that. At 97 percent of original list, sellers are giving back roughly $20,000 to $25,000 on a $750K listing to get to the closing table — meaningful negotiation room versus the 100 percent ratio at Lakewood. Buyers willing to shop the back half of Englewood listings (homes that have been on the market 30 to 60 days) can negotiate on a city where Lakewood and Centennial buyers cannot.</p>

<p>The trade-off Englewood asks you to accept is finished square footage and home age. 2,630 median sqft, often in homes built between 1955 and 1985. The wins are walkability to Englewood's downtown corridor, Light Rail access from the Englewood Station, and proximity to the Denver Tech Center for commuters. Buyers shopping the lower Englewood bands should also read the <a href="/blog/englewood-first-time-buyer-zip-code-guide-80110-80113-80111-2026">Englewood ZIP code guide</a> — the same three-ZIP pattern that defines the entry-level Englewood market also defines the top tier, just with bigger homes.</p>

<h2 id="centennial-flavor">Centennial at $750K — square footage and shopping depth</h2>

<p>$750,000 sits 15 percent above the citywide April 2026 Centennial median of $650,000. That places this band in mid-tier Centennial — not the top of the market, not the entry tier, but the strong middle. The product mix is overwhelmingly two-story single-family homes built between 1985 and 2010 in the master-planned subdivisions south of Arapahoe Road and east of I-25. Buyers tour homes with finished basements, three- and four-car garages, and lot sizes that range from 6,500 to 10,000 square feet.</p>

<p>The 3,300 median finished square feet is the headline. That is roughly 540 square feet more than Lakewood and 670 more than Englewood at the same price point. The cost is location — Centennial sits 12 to 14 miles southeast of downtown Denver, with commute times to downtown ranging from 25 to 45 minutes depending on traffic. The benefit is product, schools (Cherry Creek and Littleton Public Schools both serve parts of Centennial), and Tech Center proximity.</p>

<p>The shopping menu is the deepest of the three cities. 31 active residential listings in the $700K to $800K band as of May 3, 2026. Centennial buyers at $750K can be selective on layout, lot, and finish level in a way Lakewood and Englewood buyers cannot at the same budget. The 8-day median time in MLS shows the market is moving, but the inventory depth means buyers can usually see three to five homes before writing — not just the one that came on Friday.</p>

<h2 id="which-fits">Which city fits which $750K buyer?</h2>

<p>The honest answer is: the three suburbs do not compete on the same dimension. Each one wins on a different axis.</p>

<p><strong>Centennial fits the space-and-schools buyer.</strong> Growing family, two or more kids, two cars in the garage, room to grow. The square footage advantage is real, the shopping menu is deepest, and the Cherry Creek and Littleton school catchments anchor the buyer pool. The compromise is the 25 to 45-minute downtown commute and the master-planned-subdivision sameness — some buyers love it, some find it inert.</p>

<p><strong>Lakewood fits the foothills-and-trails buyer.</strong> Weekend access to Red Rocks, Bear Creek, and the I-70 ski corridor matters more than square footage. The 100 percent close-to-list ratio means you will write a competitive offer or you will not get the home, but the lifestyle on the west side of the metro is genuinely different — older neighborhoods, mature trees, hillier topography, faster access to the mountains. Buyers who care about that pay a PSF premium for it.</p>

<p><strong>Englewood fits the urban-edge buyer.</strong> Walkability to coffee shops, Light Rail to downtown Denver, Tech Center proximity without committing to the deep southeast suburbs. The home will be older and smaller. The neighborhood will have character that newer Centennial subdivisions do not. The 25-day median time in MLS and the 97 percent close-to-list ratio give buyers room to negotiate — which Lakewood and Centennial buyers do not get at the same band.</p>

<p>$750K does not buy the same product in any two of these cities. It buys three different homes, three different lifestyles, and three different market dynamics. The right one is whichever one you would still be happy in five years from now — when the finished square footage, the commute, and the neighborhood feel start to matter more than the negotiation room you got at the closing table.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Where does $750K buy the most house in South Denver in 2026?</h3>
<p>Centennial. April 2026 closings in the $700K to $800K band ran at a 3,300 median finished square foot count, roughly 540 square feet more than Lakewood (2,760) and 670 more than Englewood (2,630) at the same price point (REcolorado MLS, n=22 Lakewood, n=5 Englewood, n=24 Centennial). The trade-off is location: Centennial sits deeper into the southeast suburbs than Lakewood's west-metro position or Englewood's urban-edge corridor.</p>

<h3>Is $750K a top-tier or mid-tier price in each of these cities?</h3>
<p>It depends on the city. The April 2026 citywide median close price was $589,450 in Lakewood (n=190), $602,500 in Englewood (n=66), and $650,000 in Centennial (n=150). $750K sits 27 percent above the Lakewood median, 25 percent above Englewood, and 15 percent above Centennial. The same dollars buy a top-of-market home in Lakewood and Englewood, and a mid-tier family home in Centennial.</p>

<h3>How fast do $750K homes sell in each market?</h3>
<p>Median days in MLS at the $700K to $800K band ran 5 days in Lakewood, 8 days in Centennial, and 25 days in Englewood (REcolorado MLS, April 2026). Lakewood closings landed at 100 percent of original list price, Centennial at 99 percent, and Englewood at 97 percent. The Lakewood band was the tightest — buyers competed hardest there.</p>

<h3>Which $750K market has the most active inventory right now?</h3>
<p>Centennial had 31 active residential listings in the $700K to $800K band as of May 3, 2026, against 21 in Englewood and 19 in Lakewood (REcolorado MLS). Centennial buyers have the deepest shopping menu at this budget. Lakewood is the shallowest, partly because $750K is squarely top-tier there and most Lakewood inventory sits below it.</p>
